# 5. Quarterly Tax Prep

If you expect to owe tax when you file your annual return, the IRS generally
wants you to pay it during the year in quarterly installments, called estimated
tax. Employees have tax withheld from paychecks; business owners usually do not,
so estimated tax is how you stay current.

## Education only

Estimated tax rules depend on your income, filing status, state, and business
structure. This chapter explains the idea in plain English — it is not
instructions for your return. Confirm deadlines, amounts, and forms with a tax
professional and IRS.gov.

## Who usually pays estimated tax

You may need to pay estimated tax if you:

- Run a sole proprietorship, partnership, LLC, or S corporation
- Earn freelance, gig, reselling, or royalty income
- Expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax when you file (general rule of thumb —
  confirm with your tax professional)

Employees whose paychecks already cover their tax may not need to pay
estimated tax. Ask your tax professional.

## Typical deadlines (verify each year)

Estimated tax payments are usually due:

- April 15 — for January–March
- June 15 — for April–May
- September 15 — for June–August
- January 15 of the next year — for September–December

If the 15th falls on a weekend or holiday, the deadline usually moves to the
next business day. Check IRS.gov for the current year's dates.

## A simple quarterly prep routine

Every quarter, do this:

1. **Add up income** — total all payments received this quarter.
2. **Add up expenses** — total all categorized spending this quarter.
3. **Calculate net profit** — income minus expenses.
4. **Set aside money** — move a percentage of net profit to a separate savings
   account for taxes (your tax professional can suggest a percentage).
5. **Pay estimated tax** — if required, pay by the deadline using IRS.gov or the
   method your tax professional recommends.
6. **File the paperwork** — keep a copy of each payment confirmation.

## AI prompts for quarterly prep

Prompt — Quarter summary:

```
I am keeping my own small-business records. Here are my totals for the
quarter:

Total income: $___
Total expenses: $___
Net profit: $___

Create a simple quarterly summary in plain English: net profit, top expense
categories, and a reminder list of what to do next (set aside tax money,
confirm estimated tax deadline, file payment). Do not calculate tax owed and do
not give tax advice — I will verify amounts with my tax professional.
```

Prompt — Payment reminder:

```
Write a short plain-English checklist for paying U.S. federal estimated tax:
where to pay online, what information I need, and what to save for my records.
Do not tell me how much to pay — my tax professional will calculate that.
```

## If you miss a deadline

Pay as soon as you can. Penalties and interest usually depend on how late and
how much you owe. A tax professional can help you catch up and reduce future
risk.
