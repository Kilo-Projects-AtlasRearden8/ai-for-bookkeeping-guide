3. Categorizing Expenses

Expenses are what your business spends to operate. Categories are labels you put on each expense so your tax preparer (or tax software) can figure out which are deductible. AI is good at suggesting categories, but you make the final call.

Common small-business expense categories

These are common U.S. Schedule C-style categories. Your exact list may differ — ask your tax professional which ones apply to you.

CategoryExamples
AdvertisingBusiness cards, website ads, promoted posts
Office expensesPostage, printer ink, stationery
SuppliesItems you use up quickly (packing materials, pens)
RentOffice or studio space you rent
UtilitiesElectricity, internet, phone for business use
MealsBusiness meals (usually 50% deductible — ask your pro)
TravelHotels, airfare for business trips
VehicleBusiness miles, parking, gas (keep a mileage log)
Professional servicesCPA, lawyer, tax preparer fees
Software & subscriptionsAccounting software, AI tools, website hosting
Shipping & deliveryPostage to send products to customers
Cost of goods soldItems you buy to resell (books, inventory)
Home officePortion of home expenses if you qualify (ask your pro)

"Ordinary and necessary" is the standard phrase: an expense is ordinary if it is common in your line of work, and necessary if it is helpful and appropriate. Ask your tax professional before you deduct anything unusual.

How to categorize with AI

Paste a list of expenses and let AI suggest categories, then review every suggestion yourself. AI does not know your business as well as you do.

Prompt — Categorize expenses
I am keeping my own small-business bookkeeping records. Below is a list of
expenses. For each one, suggest the most likely category from this list:
Advertising, Office expenses, Supplies, Rent, Utilities, Meals, Travel,
Vehicle, Professional services, Software & subscriptions, Shipping,
Cost of goods sold, Home office, Other.

Mark anything unclear with [?]. Do not give tax advice — I will verify with my
tax professional.

Expenses:
...

Review AI's suggestions

AI might label a lunch with a friend as "Meals" when it was personal. It might call a new laptop "Supplies" when your tax pro prefers it tracked as equipment. Read every row and change anything that does not fit your business.

Keep receipts

A category label is not proof of spending. Keep the receipt, invoice, or bank record for every expense (Chapter 4).

Expense worksheet

Use the Monthly Expense Tracker worksheet (site/worksheets/expense-tracker.html, or docs/worksheets/worksheet-expense-tracker.md). Print one per month.

Mileage tip

If you drive for business, track miles in a simple log: date, start and end addresses, purpose, and miles. The IRS lets you deduct business miles at a standard rate that changes each year — check IRS.gov for the current rate and ask your tax professional how to use it.
