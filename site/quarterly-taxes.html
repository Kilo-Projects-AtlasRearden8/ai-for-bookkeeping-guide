5. Quarterly Tax Prep

If you expect to owe tax when you file your annual return, the IRS generally wants you to pay it during the year in quarterly installments, called estimated tax. Employees have tax withheld from paychecks; business owners usually do not, so estimated tax is how you stay current.

Education only. Estimated tax rules depend on your income, filing status, state, and business structure. This chapter explains the idea in plain English — it is not instructions for your return. Confirm deadlines, amounts, and forms with a tax professional and IRS.gov.

Who usually pays estimated tax

You may need to pay estimated tax if you:

  • Run a sole proprietorship, partnership, LLC, or S corporation
  • Earn freelance, gig, reselling, or royalty income
  • Expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax when you file (general rule of thumb — confirm with your tax professional)

Employees whose paychecks already cover their tax may not need to pay estimated tax. Ask your tax professional.

If the 15th falls on a weekend or holiday, the deadline usually moves to the next business day. Check IRS.gov for the current year's dates.

A simple quarterly prep routine

  1. Add up income — total all payments received this quarter.
  2. Add up expenses — total all categorized spending this quarter.
  3. Calculate net profit — income minus expenses.
  4. Set aside money — move a percentage of net profit to a separate savings account for taxes (your tax professional can suggest a percentage).
  5. Pay estimated tax — if required, pay by the deadline using IRS.gov or the method your tax professional recommends.
  6. File the paperwork — keep a copy of each payment confirmation.

Quarterly tax prep worksheet

Use the Quarterly Tax Prep Checklist worksheet (site/worksheets/quarterly-tax-checklist.html, or docs/worksheets/worksheet-quarterly-tax-checklist.md). Fill it out four times a year.

Total expenses: $___

Create a simple quarterly summary in plain English: net profit, top expense

confirm estimated tax deadline, file payment). Do not calculate tax owed and do
not give tax advice — I will verify amounts with my tax professional.
Prompt — Payment reminder
Write a short plain-English checklist for paying U.S. federal estimated tax:
where to pay online, what information I need, and what to save for my records.
Do not tell me how much to pay — my tax professional will calculate that.

If you miss a deadline

Pay as soon as you can. Penalties and interest usually depend on how late and how much you owe. A tax professional can help you catch up and reduce future risk.
