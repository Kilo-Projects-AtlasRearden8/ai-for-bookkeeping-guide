2. Tracking Income

Income is every dollar your business receives. Tracking it means recording each payment, where it came from, and when it arrived. Good income records protect you if the IRS ever asks, and they make quarterly tax prep much easier.

Record every payment, even small ones

If you sell a $12 book, record the $12. Do not wait until the end of the year and try to remember. Common income sources for small businesses:

  • Client payments (check, Venmo, PayPal, Stripe, bank transfer)
  • Book royalties (KDP, other publishers)
  • Reselling sales (eBay, Amazon, Poshmark, local markets)
  • Affiliate or referral commissions
  • Tips or gratuities related to the business

Use one row per payment. Do not lump a whole month into one line.

Separate business and personal money

Open a separate bank account for your business if you can. Never pay personal grocery bills from the business account, and do not deposit customer checks into your personal account. Mixing money makes bookkeeping harder and can erase legal protections for an LLC.

Handling fees

Payment platforms often take a fee. Record the gross amount the customer paid as income, and record the fee as an expense (for example, "Payment processing fees"). Do not record only what landed in your bank account.
